# Community view

The **Community** button (or `?mode=community`) lifts the camera over Kukuiʻula. The view flies in from high over the sea to the home, and you can orbit, pan and zoom across the whole community. It shows:
- real terrain;
- the reef in its surveyed depths (2013);
- roads, paths, pools, ponds, beaches and golf features;
- buildings and trees;
- pins for the places around.

Pick a pin to fly there. Its card gives the place's category, its straight-line distance from the home and its source. The live sky still runs, so the sun, the shadows and the time of day all work here too. Code: `src/community.js`. Data bake: `scripts/geo_community.py`.

**What it is:** a concept visualization over a public-domain aerial photo (USDA NAIP, flown 11 February 2022) and open public data. It is not the developer's map, and nothing in it is copied from kukuiula.com. The home is this project's concept model, placed to ±25 m (register I-9). A soft gold tint with a feathered edge marks its modelled lot, and the home's card says what it is: the modelled lot, with an assumed size (61.5 × 184.4 ft, register I-6). It is not a surveyed lot line, and none is drawn. The home's pin reads "Nā Hiku concept home". The other thirteen Nā Hiku homes are shown as assumed copies of this design (register I-14). They stand on a lawn the bake draws over the modelled row (tinted like the photo's turf); in 2025–26 imagery that strip is graded, unbuilt land, and the 2022 photo predates that grading. No surveyed lot lines, neighbourhood boundaries or views from the lot are shown or claimed.

## The aerial photo
The land is the **USDA NAIP Hawaiʻi 2021–23** 4-band photo (red, green, blue, near-infrared; 0.6 m), USDA Farm Service Agency, distributed by NOAA Digital Coast (dataset 9668, InPort item 69406). The box was flown on 11 February 2022. The InPort record (checked 19 Oct 2026) says: "Access Constraints: There are no limitations for access. Use Constraints: None. The USDA-FSA Aerial Photography Field office asks to be credited in derived products." It is credited as "Aerial photo: USDA NAIP 2022".
- **Fetch** (`scripts/geo_naip.py`): only the quarter-quad tiles that touch the box with the bake's 100 m margin: `m_2115904_se`, `m_2115905_sw` and `m_2115913_nw` (2022-02-11; two of them are also listed under 2022-02-12 as identical files, fetched once). They are reprojected from NAD83 / UTM 4N to WGS84 / UTM 4N (EPSG:32604), cubic, onto the bake's own texel grid (0.488 m), and feathered over 15 m where they overlap, into `<cache>/naip_comm.npy`. No tile covers the box's south-west corner (open sea), so 2.3% of the grid has no photo.
- **Colour match** (`Inputs._photo`): per band a curve `g · (DN/255)^γ` fitted to the Sentinel-2 median mosaic over stable land at 10 m (γ 1.2 / 1.4 / 1.6 for red, green, blue), then a smooth gain field (about 250 m, 0.89–1.14) that takes out what is left between flight lines and toward the tiles' edges, and a saturation matched to the satellite's. Places that changed since 2022 (graded land, new buildings) differ too much to count and are left out of the fit. The outer 150 m of the box still hand over to the satellite colour, which the matched photo already meets.
- **Cloud:** a few thin wisps (about 3 ha, over the solar farm in the north-east and the pasture in the north-west) are found as much brighter, greyer and bluer than the satellite sees the place. Under them the photo keeps its detail (contrast restored) on the satellite's broad colour.
- **Glint:** the photo is used on land only, so the sea's sun glint never enters; the ponds drawn as water keep their drawn colour.
- **What stays drawn:** the sea and the reef (colour from the surveyed depth), the ponds drawn as water, the modelled Nā Hiku row (one lawn, tinted from the photo's mown turf nearby and given the photo's fine grain at half contrast, no mowing stripes), and the ground past the photo's edge. The drawn layers below still decide the land-cover class the view's close-range grain reads (`w_*.png` B), and the lidar normals, the canopy's cast heights and the traced shadows are as before.
- **Shading:** the photo holds the flight's own sun and shadows (late morning, February). The bake keeps only a quarter of its occlusion on photo pixels, and the view eases its live shading there (`w_*.png` G on land, below): 70% of the lidar normals' relief, 75% of the traced shadows, the crowns' relief and most of the close-range grain and mottle. The 10 m mesh still takes the live sun, so the hills keep their light.
- **Age:** the photo predates the 2025–26 grading on the Nā Hiku strip and some newer buildings; the 3D context buildings come from the map and can stand where the photo shows bare ground.

Before the photo, the ground was drawn from the layers below alone (`COMM_NO_PHOTO=1` still bakes that way). The state's Vivid imagery (Maxar), the USGS 2010 Kauaʻi orthoimagery (DigitalGlobe licence) and the state's WorldView-2 layer are not used: their licences restrict commercial use.

At every width a short credits line names the main sources with the licences linked, and a Credits button opens the full list, built from `community.json` `attribution` and `places.json` `attribution`. On wide screens the short line reads "Aerial photo: USDA NAIP 2022 · © OpenStreetMap contributors, Microsoft, TomTom (ODbL) via Overture · Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data 2023–26 · USGS · USACE/NOAA · Meta/WRI canopy CC BY 4.0" (on a landscape phone over two lines); on portrait phones (≤600 px wide), where it must fit one line, it keeps "Photo: USDA NAIP 2022 · © OpenStreetMap contributors · Meta/WRI CC BY 4.0", and the Copernicus notice is in the Credits list.

**Drawn, not measured** (on land most of this now lies under the photo, and shows only where the photo does not, or as the land-cover class):
- Surface texture: grass grain, clods, sand ripples, leaf clumps, asphalt grain and mower stripes.
- Roads: a light, warm, weathered asphalt (about 10% reflectance, patched, the main roads a little darker, the wheel tracks a little darker than the edges) with a soft verge of worn grass about a metre wide on each side, instead of a dark ribbon with a pale kerb line. Driveways and cart paths are concrete, the cart paths shaded where trees stand beside them; footpaths are muted concrete, and unpaved paths are drawn as worn ground.
- Crown colour: picked per crown-sized patch (5 m Worley cells) from a small leaf palette (deep green, olive, grey-green, a fresher green, dark), with jitter and only a light pull toward the place's own tint. It no longer follows the 10 m ground colour under the crown, which drew pasture-green and soil-coloured crowns. The canopy map is rounded off over 0.5 m, so crown edges are not stair-stepped, and thin lines of "canopy" on bare ground (under about 3 m across, over 6 m long, NDVI under 0.25: fences, walls and pad edges) are dropped.
- Golf: a first cut, a ring of slightly longer, darker grass about 2.5 m wide, around every fairway; the inferred fairways' edges are crisp and anti-aliased (about 0.8 m), and the fairway green is about 13% less saturated and a little deeper and bluer (a natural green, not lime).
- Red earth is a weathered ochre-red rather than rust, with a band of dry grass where it thins into the grass.
- No breaking surf inside the Kukuiʻula Small Boat Harbor's breakwater: sea within about 380 m of the harbour whose line of sight toward the south swell (from 165–240°, out to 400 m) runs into land is calm, with a soft edge toward the harbour mouth and only a little lapping at its shore. The view's moving surf reads the same mask (`w_*.png` G on the sea).
- The crisp edges between covers. They follow the satellite's own 10 m boundaries, roughened at the metre scale. Red earth's edges are softened over about 2 m and broken up, as grass thins into dirt.
- A floor on the tone of crowns: their colour is kept at about 4.5% reflectance in green or above, and under crowns and thickets the broad (9 m) baked occlusion is softened and nothing ends darker than about 4.5%, so tree-filled gullies keep their shape but read as forest rather than black under the live shadows.
- A rough canopy surface given to dense scrub, so that it takes light and shade.
- Lone crowns (canopy patches under 150 m²) keep only a fifth of their rounded relief in the normals and 40% in the baked occlusion: the view stands a 3D tree on each, so the ground is not also raised into a shaded mound under it. Their traced shadow stays, and woods keep their full rounded canopy in the bake (the viewer halves every crown's relief, below).
- The modelled Nā Hiku row (the walkthrough's neighbourhood rectangle): one striped lawn under the 3D homes, over land that is bare graded soil in the 2025–26 imagery. It is tinted like the grass around it and feathered into the ground over about 8 m with a noise-broken edge (its stripes fade out toward it), and its flattened normals ease into the lidar's over the same band, so there is no rectangle and no edge per lot.
- The seafloor in the filled survey gaps: depth from the survey around each gap, and as rough as the reef around it.
- The ground in the lidar's voids inland (among them the graded pad just north of the Nā Hiku strip): the 2 m DEM's broad shape, bent to meet the lidar at the void's rim. A broad dip inside a void keeps only about half its depth.
- 3D buildings: footprints are mapped (OSM, Microsoft), but almost all heights are guessed from footprint area (10.5 ft a storey: one storey 10.5 ft, two 21 ft, three 31.5 ft; only about 0.6% of the footprints carry a height or floor count), roofs are drawn hipped, and roof colour is the 10 m satellite pixel snapped to one of five finishes.

**Kukuiʻula's golf turf is inferred.** Inside the Kukuiʻula course, OpenStreetMap maps the outline, its ponds, one green and the driving range, but no fairways, tees or bunkers. On a course where mapped fairways and driving ranges cover less than 5% of it, fairways are drawn where the 10 m colour is lighter than the course around them (60 m context), on tree-free, mown-looking grass. That applies to Kukuiʻula; Kiahuna's fairways are mapped and drawn from the map. Greens, tees, bunkers and driving ranges are drawn wherever they are mapped, at Kukuiʻula too.

**Mapped ponds that are no longer open water are drawn as land.** OpenStreetMap still maps reservoirs that have grown over: four in the gulches 1.9–2.4 km north-west and north of the home, one about 550 m north-north-east of it where trees stand in the outline, and a small pond under trees. It also maps a golf water-hazard outline along the shore about 700 m south-south-west of the home that is mown grass, and a pond near the coast about 1.4 km south-east of the home where the satellite sees land. A mapped pond is drawn as water only if both of these hold:
- trees (canopy height over 2 m) cover less than 15% of it;
- it is not both green in the satellite colour (Sentinel-2 NDVI median over 0.3; open water is below 0) and as bright as its banks in the near-infrared (median at least 0.75 of the land 10–30 m around it).

Water absorbs near-infrared whatever grows in it. Kukuiʻula's golf ponds are green with algae and only a few 10 m pixels across, so they read 0.35–0.7 in NDVI, but their near-infrared is between a fifth and three quarters of their banks', and they stay water. Ponds too small to hold a whole 10 m pixel are judged by the canopy alone. Eight outlines are left as land this way: they get no water paint, no pond water in `w_*.png` and no levelling in `h.bin`, and the ground there is drawn from the colour and the canopy like the land around it. Inside the ponds drawn as water, the canopy map's crowns are removed, so no crown, crown shading or shadow stands in the water.

## Tiles
The tiles are north-up UTM 4N squares of 1 km over E 447 500–453 500, N 2 418 000–2 423 000. That runs from Lāwaʻi Bay to Poʻipū, and from the sea to the Kōloa bypass. `geodata/community/community.json` gives the box, the transform to the walkthrough's world frame (feet), the channel encodings (`encoding`, and `sdfPerM`: the sea distance's steps per metre in `w_*.png`), the open sea's measured colour (`deepSea`, linear; the viewer draws the open sea in a lifted cobalt, below) and the credits.

| File | Size | Content |

| `s_c_r.webp` | 512² | The same albedo at 2 m per texel, loaded first and used for distant tiles |
| `n_c_r.webp` | 1024², lossless | Lidar ground and crown normals (east, north), and the height of what casts shadow: canopy and eaves, 0.1 m steps |
| `w_c_r.png` | 512² | R: signed distance to the sea's edge, 128 + 1 per metre (sea positive, ±127 m; `sdfPerM`). G: 255 in the ponds drawn as water; on other land 7 × the aerial photo's weight (0–7, under the ponds' threshold); on the sea, 80 × the harbour's calm (0–1), which stays under the view's pond threshold (0.4). B: sea depth, 10 per metre; on land more than 4 m from the sea and off the ponds (R < 124, G < 8), the land cover: 16 × class + dryness. Classes (`community.json` `encoding.landCover`): 0 none, 1 turf (fairways, greens, tees, first cut, golf rough, lawns, the modelled row), 2 grass (pasture), 3 scrub, 4 canopy, 5 soil (red earth, tracks, dirt paths), 6 rock, 7 sand (beaches, bunkers), 8 paved (roads, drives, cart paths, roofs, pools). Dryness runs 0–15 from the 10 m NDVI (0 at 0.62 and over, 15 at 0.22 and under). Each texel takes the class that covers most of it in the bake's own drawing order |
| `h.bin` | 1200 × 1000 | Uint16 ground heights, 5 m, world feet; each pond drawn as water level at its lidar water surface, no higher than its bank. The hosted copy carries it as `h.b64.txt` |

## In the viewer
- **Ground:** each tile is a 10 m mesh (20 m on low quality). Inside the modelled neighbourhood's rectangle it carries that neighbourhood's heights, easing into the real ground over 300 ft; its outer 150 m eases into the walkthrough's real-site terrain. While the view is open, the walkthrough's own lot ground is hidden and the tiles carry the ground everywhere, so there is no hole. Inside the tile box the walkthrough's coarse real-site terrain is sunk below the tiles rather than clipped, so its valleys, which sit above the lidar ground, cannot show through; beyond the box it carries the ground as before. Both are restored on exit.
- **3D trees:** the view's own trees (**Trees**, below) stand on the surface the tiles draw under them: their 10 m triangles (20 m on low quality) over `h.bin`, not the smooth 5 m height function between the triangles' corners, which is up to about 8 ft off them on a gulch's edge (about 9 ft on low quality); off the tiles they stand on that function. So no trunk is buried or left standing in the air. The walkthrough's trees (`src/realsite.js` `trees()`) are hidden while the view is open and are never moved.
- **The modelled lot:** a soft gold tint with a feathered edge, drawn on the ground by the tile shader.
- **Shading:** the tile shader replaces the normals with the lidar's and traces each pixel's shadow across the height map toward the live sun (18 steps, up to about 30 m; 8 coarser steps, about 25 m, on low quality). Both fade out only at an edge a tile shares with a tile that has only its 2 m albedo, so no lighting seam shows there.
- **Water:** the sea and the ponds drawn as water get glassy, wind-rippled water. Swell rolls in over the shallow reef, its sets following the distance from the shore (stored out to 127 m), and breaks where it is shallowest; the swash moves at the shore. Its sun highlights are capped and its roughness has a floor (0.12 near, 0.5 far), so the ripples never sparkle into white specks. Inside the harbour's breakwater (`w_*.png` G on the sea) no swell breaks and the swash is cut to 30%.
- **The open sea, drawn:** the measured deep water (`deepSea`, about 0.003 / 0.009 / 0.025 linear) reads near black from the air, so the viewer draws the open sea in a cobalt lifted from it (0.006 / 0.025 / 0.09 linear, `uDeep` in `src/community.js`). Water deeper than about 10 m hands over to it by 24 m (the stored depth stops at 25.5 m, and the albedo's dark streaks run on below that); over the last 300 m of the box the whole sea takes it. The reef above 10 m keeps its baked colour.
- **Ponds:** the water a shade lighter, close up a narrow shelf of lighter, greener water along the bank (the pond channel blurred over about 4 m; it fades out between about 1,500 and 4,000 ft away, where it would only grey the pond), a teal-grey margin where the water meets its bank (the channel's anti-aliased edge, held off the sea and its last 3 m of shore, so a G the bake writes on the sea, such as a harbour's calm, never reads as a pond's bank), and a little more of the sky in its reflection, so they read as water rather than holes.
- **Ground up close:** within about 4,200 ft of the camera (2,600 ft on low quality), land well inside the coast gets grain by its land cover (`w_*.png` B on land): tussocks and straw-coloured dry tips on grass (by the dryness), a fine even grain on turf, bush clumps with dark gaps on scrub, leaf clumps on canopy, clods on soil, speckle and cracks on rock, a faint grain on sand. Scrub, canopy, rock and grass also get a little relief for the live sun (not on low quality). The four texels around each pixel are fetched and weighted by hand, since a filtered class means nothing; the grain fades out with distance and with the size of a pixel on the ground, so it never shimmers. Tiles baked without the class are drawn as before.
- **The sea beyond the tiles:** while the view is open, the walkthrough's sea beyond the tiles has the mosaic's glint and haze offset taken off, and all but its bright shallows take the same cobalt, so it meets the tiles' sea; the open-sea skirt to the horizon takes it too. The offset is read from the mosaic's smoothed red channel and is taken off only water near sea level. It is restored on exit.
- **The edge of the world:** inland (within about 65° of north from the home), the walkthrough's far terrain fades over its outer 4 km into what lies behind it: above the horizon the sky (the sky texture and that frame's cumulus, so the dome shows through), below it the haze itself (the scene's fog colour, which distant land fades into anyway, and which the far sea also ends in); the sea skirt beyond it takes the same haze, and so does the dome below the horizon. The island goes into haze instead of ending at a straight edge with a flat sea-level plane beyond, and the band under the horizon is no brighter than the sky above it. Seaward the skirt keeps a crisp sea horizon. Only while the view is open (`src/realsite.js`, gated by `SEA_LIFT`; the haze colour is `SKY_U.haze` in `src/skydome.js`, set by `src/daylight.js`).
- **Streaming:** the tiles that stand largest on the screen stream in their 2048 px albedo and 1024 px normals (12 tiles); on low quality 4 tiles, at 1024 and 512 px. The rest stay at 2 m. Tiles are ranked by the distance to their nearest point (a tile the camera hangs over counts as near, however far its centre), and tiles out of view come far behind those in it. Toward an edge shared with a 2 m tile, over 220 m, the lidar normals and the traced shadows fade out and the albedo eases into the tile's own 2 m albedo (`s_c_r.webp`, which stays on the GPU), the very texture its neighbour shows: at the shared edge both sides draw the same pixels, so the hand-over is a gradient, not a seam.
- **The Nā Hiku row from the air** (restored on exit):
  - the standing seams' ribs and their hip and ridge caps are hidden, and the roof pans go matt with their seam normal map eased and no metal: from the air the sub-pixel ribs aliased into white speckle. Gutters, flashing, fascia and gable vents lose their metal too;
  - the pools are a deeper aqua (`#1d7f93`) and reflect less sky;
  - the modelled access road is a warm mid-grey asphalt (about 10% reflectance), not a near-black slab;
  - the sun's shadow map covers the whole row (a square about 690 ft from its centre, instead of 110 ft round the lot), so every home, palm and garden casts its shadow; only the row casts into it, since the tiles already trace the shadows of the trees and the context buildings;
  - our modelled home (Lot 25) takes a warm light on its roof by day, fading out with the sun's altitude, and the thirteen assumed copies' roofs sit a little darker, so it reads first under its pin. Nothing is drawn on the ground around any lot: no outline and no glow.
- **Context buildings:** calm massing rather than boxes with window grids. The walls lose the window pattern by day (the lit windows of night stay) and take a warm off-white a little below paper white. The roofs take a muted palette mapped from the five snapped finishes by brightness (dark grey from about 11% reflectance, warm grey, a light warm grey and, for the red ones, a weathered terracotta; the green-grey finish, mostly a crown's colour snapped onto a roof, keeps only a tenth of its hue; none pitch black or white) and are matt. They are drawn on a second roof geometry built at load: a hip rising more than 10 ft is eased to 30% above that (a 200 ft lodge carried a 42 ft pyramid whose shaded half read near black), so houses keep their roofs and lodges and the clubhouse get low hips. They cast no shadow map here (the tiles trace their shadows from the baked eave heights). On desktop a contact-shadow pass (GTAO, about a 22 ft radius, within about 4000 ft of the view's target; main.js switches it off for this view and the view switches it back on) grounds the buildings, trees and homes. All restored on exit.
- **Trees:** the same measured trees, drawn as the view's own set (the walkthrough's trees are hidden meanwhile and are unchanged): three crown forms chosen by each tree's height to width and a hash (a broad umbrella on a short thick trunk, a rounded dome, an uneven narrower crown; the dome and the uneven crown come down near the ground with no trunk, so no thin stalk shows from the air), coloured from a small leaf palette (deep green, a fresh yellow-green, olive, grey-green) with jitter and a light pull toward the measured colour, a little more sky light on their shaded side, and no shadow map (the tiles trace their shadows).
- **Crown relief and steep banks:** the crowns' colour is the bake's (its leaf palette and tone floor, above). On full-detail tiles every crown's baked relief in the normals (cast height over about 0.3–1.4 m on land) is halved, since thickets read as bubble wrap from the air; a lone crown, which the bake already cut to a fifth, keeps a tenth. The lidar's steepest metre-scale walls (tilted more than about 40-70 degrees from the vertical, such as a ditch's or gulch's banks) have their tilt eased by half toward the 10 m ground's and, in shade by day, take 30% of the sun's light as the light the sunlit ground throws into a narrow cut: their shaded bank read as a black, beaded crack from the air. It is one block in `src/community.js` (`CM_RELIEF`; define `CM_NO_RELIEF` to drop it).
- **Light and grade (a sunny trade-wind day):** while the view is open, `src/daylight.js` applies the view's look (`main.js` switches it with the mode; nothing else changes it, and leaving the view gives every mode its own light back):
  - the haze thinned to 55% from the air, and by day tinted a light sky blue (at the haze's own brightness) instead of the horizon's grey; the low sky (within about 15° of the horizon) takes the same hue, so distant land fades into a sky of its own colour instead of standing out as a blue cut-out against a grey band;
  - the exposure raised by 40% by day (18% at night): an eye adapted to a bright sea and sky;
  - by day the sun a touch warmer (luminance kept), twice the sky's hemisphere fill, and a little bloom on highlights (not on low quality);
  - a display grade after the tone mapping (`src/grade.js`): darks lifted a little, vibrance (dull colours gain saturation, vivid ones hardly any), olive greens pulled a few degrees toward yellow-green with a little more colour and light, pale blues (sky, haze, sea) a little deeper;
  - the sky: rounder cumulus tops (`uDome` 0.3 against 0.06), each ray marched at most 4.5 km into the deck (low rays marched whole smeared each cloud into a column), no cirrus, at most about a quarter of the sky in cloud unless it is overcast, the deck thinned out between 16 and 42 km away (`uCloudFade`; elsewhere 70-125 km), so the far cumulus no longer piles into a grey band just above the sea horizon, and no cloud bank over the mountains (its flat top read as a flat-topped mountain).
  
  The daytime parts fade out as the sun goes down (from 14° above the horizon to 1° below), so dusk, night and the night sky look as they do elsewhere.
- **The hero frame:** the flight in ends inland of the home (north-north-west of it on wide screens, north-west in phone portrait, about north in landscape), looking over the golf course and the home to the coast, the reef and the sea, with the horizon about a third down and sky above it, and the sun from the side. The home sits on a lower third point of the part of the screen the chrome leaves free (below the header, above a phone's bottom card, right of the card where it stands open as a column, left of a place's card standing at the right); the target is solved for that with the view's own projection, so it holds on desktop, in phone portrait (closer, the home near the middle) and in landscape. Where the open card stands as a column on the left (wide screens) the view is centred in the space to its right, and it glides back to the full width as the card folds; on small screens the card sits at the bottom and the view keeps the full width (a landscape phone's folded card is a strip at the bottom left; its open sheet stands as a column there, which the home's frame keeps clear of). A picked place lands in the middle of the free part of the screen, beside its own card; the home's pin gives the frame above, clear of the home's card.
- **The photo's pixels** (`w_*.png` G on land): a grade (red ×1.07, green ×1.10, blue ×0.92, then saturation ×1.05), since the photo matched to the satellite's median read a little dark, grey and blue beside the sea and sky; the lidar normals keep 30% of their relief there, the traced shadows 25%, the crowns' extra relief none, and the close-range grain 40% and the mottle 25%: the photo already holds the flight's sun and shadows.
- **Notice:** one line stays under the title at every width, in gold small caps on a soft scrim: "Independent concept · Nā Hiku homes modeled · not by the developer · views not shown or verified" (index.html `#concept .cc-cm`; the other views keep their own longer line). At golden hour and dusk (sun from about 16° down to -4°, easing out by -10°), when the low sky behind the header is bright, the scrim deepens (`--cm-scrim`, set from the sun's altitude in `update()` and cleared on exit), so the line keeps its contrast. The card says the rest in two sentences ("Aerial photo: USDA NAIP 2022, over open map data; the Nā Hiku homes are modeled. Places and distances are approximate; views from the home are not shown or verified."), and its "How this was made" toggle holds the full text: not by the developer, not the developer's map, not construction documentation; the photo (February 2022, colour-matched to Sentinel-2, predating the 2025–26 grading on the strip and some newer buildings) and the other sources; the home as a concept model on a lot of assumed size with no lot lines drawn; and the other Nā Hiku homes as assumed copies of this design on a strip that is graded, unbuilt land in 2025–26 imagery. The home's pin card says the same.
- **Credits:** the short line and the Credits button sit at the bottom right (on phones, along the bottom); on wider screens the line starts "Aerial photo: USDA NAIP 2022" ("Photo: USDA NAIP 2022" on phones); the Credits panel lists every source with its licence linked, and the weather's when it is live.
- **Card:** "Around the home": the place filters, "Center on home", "Whole community", the two sentences and "How this was made". It folds to its title at every width (tap the title). On wide screens it opens docked at the left, and the view is framed beside it; it folds once the flight in ends or the viewer takes the map (unless they have opened or closed it themselves), and the frame glides back to the full width. On wide screens the open card and the open sun panel share the left side: opening the sun panel folds the card, and opening the card closes the sun panel. At every width the card's title stays at its top as the card scrolls (on wide screens it turns opaque over a hairline once scrolled), and "How this was made" scrolls the card so the toggle sits just under the title. On small screens (≤600 px wide or ≤480 px tall) it is a sheet at the bottom, folded from the start (and folded again when a window shrinks to that size), with the sun control at the right of its title row (on a landscape phone it sits bottom left, with the credits bottom right); a phone keeps about 70 px of chrome at the bottom and about 85 px at the top. The card's and the pin card's titles are set in Newsreader, with a system serif until it arrives or if it cannot. Its Google Fonts stylesheet is added to the page when the view is first built (`buildUI()`), never in index.html, so the walkthrough, the fly-over and the model view make no request beyond the page's own server (`scripts/inspect_qa.mjs` checks this for the model view); the community view itself contacts fonts.googleapis.com and fonts.gstatic.com.
- **Toolbar:** one primary button, "‹ The home" (back to the walkthrough), and a "⋯" menu with Night sky (or Daylight), Fly over, Model, 360° tour and Quality (Standard or Ultra; not on phones). The menu's items press the walkthrough's own toolbar buttons, which are hidden while the view is open, so the other views' toolbars are as they were. Both buttons are 40 px with 44 px to tap. From the keyboard, Enter or the down arrow on "⋯" opens the menu with the first item focused; the arrow keys, Home and End move through it, and Esc closes it. The hosted copy (`scripts/build_artifact.py`) drops the 360° tour from the toolbar and the menu (it carries no panoramas) and hides Quality in both (it cannot reload with a query string); `main.js` `openMenu()` also hides them there.
- **Sun control:** the time panel's pill is labelled "Sun position" and shows no temperature here, so it does not read as a forecast; it opens the same day and time panel as in the walkthrough. Dates read "Sun, Jun 21" here; the other views keep "Sun 21 Jun" (the pill holds both, and index.html shows one by `body.community-mode`).
- **Labels:** pins are HTML labels. Overlapping ones give way by priority and distance, and each category can be switched off. They keep out of the header band (the full width down to the title, notice and toolbar), 16 px off the panels and the screen's edges, 6 px off each other and 12 px off the home's label; a place whose dot falls under a panel or the band is not labelled. Leaders come in two lengths, 48 px for nearer places and 24 px for farther ones, and take the other length when theirs would collide.

## Rebuild
```
python3 scripts/geo_dem1m.py          # USGS 1 m DEM over the box  -> <cache>/dem_comm_1m.npy
python3 scripts/geo_bathy.py          # USACE 2013 topobathy        -> <cache>/bathy_comm_1m.npy
python3 scripts/geo_overture.py <cache> base/land -159.515 21.86 -159.445 21.915   # beaches, bare rock
python3 scripts/geo_naip.py           # USDA NAIP 2022 photo over the box -> <cache>/naip_comm.npy (about 2 GB of tiles fetched)
python3 scripts/geo_community.py      # all 30 tiles (about 3 min each), heights, community.json
python3 scripts/geo_community.py --only "2,2;1,2"   # some tiles (community.json lists the tiles on disk)
# two halves side by side (4 CPUs): --only with the tiles whose c + r is even, and another with the odd ones;
# each rewrites h.bin and community.json, identically, when it ends
node scripts/community_qa.mjs [out]   # screens: opening, establishing, overview, home, coast, golf, trees east of the strip
# env: W=390 H=844 a phone viewport, Q=low the phone quality (CM_LOW), SHOTS=1,3 some poses only. It also checks that the
# view's 3D trees stand on the tiles (the umbrellas' trunks), that the walkthrough's trees are hidden in the view and back,
# unmoved, after leaving it, and that entering again draws the same trees.
# It also checks that the notice line and the short credits are on screen.
```
The other inputs (`s2_aoi.npz`, `dem_near_2m.npy`, `ov_*.json`, the canopy tile, `features.json`) are those of the real-site bake (`geodata/SOURCES.txt`). `places.json` is written by hand from OSM and Overture records.
