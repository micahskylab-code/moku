What Oʻahu homes actually sold for

Sold prices in Hawaiʻi usually hide behind an agent login. Moku obtained the City & County of Honolulu's recorded-sales file through a public-records request — so you can look up the last recorded sale for just about any Oʻahu home, free.

What you can look up

Search any Honolulu-county address or TMK, or tap a parcel on the map, and Moku shows its last recorded arm's-length sale — the price and the month — right on the record, next to the county-assessed value, estimated property tax, rent benchmark and hazard zones.

  • 110,000+ Oʻahu parcels with a recorded arm's-length sale on file.
  • Recorded sales through September 2025 (the county's most recent full extract).
  • Whole island — Honolulu, Kailua, Kāneʻohe, ʻEwa, Kapolei, the North Shore and West side.

The island's market number

For the overall benchmark, the State (DBEDT) puts Oʻahu's single-family median at about $1.18M (Q1 2026). That's the county-wide figure; each home's last recorded sale — one tap away on the map — is what that specific home actually traded for, which is usually the number that matters.

Honest fine print

These are recorded fee-simple conveyances from the county's public sales extract — real transactions, not listings and not estimates, the same records title companies and appraisers pull. Moku filters to arm's-length sales (dropping $1 family transfers, leases and non-market deals) and shows the most recent one per parcel. County records lag the live market by months, and the extract is current through September 2025 — always confirm with the county before relying on a figure for a transaction.

Maui recorded sales are on the map too. Kauaʻi and Hawaiʻi Island go on the same way, as their records come available.
