02 · What happened, and why

Nine chapters: what the data shows, and our read of why

2006–2007The top of the first boom
  • Oʻahu recorded 10,421 sales in 2006, a level not seen again until 2021. Maui recorded 2,331.
  • Oʻahu's price index peaked in 2006 at 95.3 (2015 = 100). Maui's single-family median averaged $702K that year.
  • Mortgage rates averaged 6.41% and 6.34%, about where they are today. The state authorized 7,549 new homes in 2006, the most in 20 years.
  • The neighbor islands were gaining households: in 2004–05 the Big Island took in 1,126 more households from other states than it lost.
Our read: easy credit and a national housing boom. Mainland households were moving to the neighbor islands (IRS shows net gains there), while Oʻahu was already losing slightly more households than it gained.
2008–2011The crash
  • Sales fell 40% on Oʻahu from 2006 to 2009 (to 6,263) and 34% on Maui (to 1,528).
  • Maui's single-family median fell 37%, from $702K (2006) to $439K (2011). Oʻahu's price index fell 14%, from 95.3 to 82.3.
  • New-home permits collapsed to 2,592 in 2009. Rates fell from 6.04% to 4.46%, but credit was tight.
  • On the neighbor islands, migration nearly stopped: the net flow was a few hundred households a year either way. Visitor arrivals by air bottomed at 6.42 million in 2009.
Our read: the national financial crisis. Hawaiʻi's neighbor islands, which depend most on outside buyers and visitors, fell hardest. Oʻahu, with its military and government base, fell least.
2012–2015Recovery, and the foreign buyer
  • Rates hit 3.66% in 2012. Oʻahu's price index rose 21%, from 82.3 (2011) to 100 (2015).
  • International visitors rose from 2.05 million (2011) to 2.78 million (2015). Foreign buyers took 4.1% of sales in 2016, and 928 sales in 2018 at the peak.
  • The neighbor islands gained households again, and the arrivals were richer. On Kauaʻi in 2012–13, households moving in averaged $103K in income, against $47K for those leaving.
Our read: cheap money, a strong yen (2011–12) and a Canadian dollar near par drew Asian and Canadian buyers. Higher-income mainland households began replacing departing locals on the neighbor islands.
2016–2019The steady climb
  • About 19,400–20,500 sales a year. Mainland buyers took 3,698–3,956 of them, between 18.6% and 19.4%.
  • Foreign buying peaked in 2018 at 928 sales (4.5%), averaging $1.25M.
  • Mortgage rates averaged 3.65–4.54%. Visitor arrivals by air reached 10.24 million in 2019.
  • Oʻahu started losing 3,300–7,700 households a year to other states. Maui's mainland buyer share slid from 42% (2015) to 28% (2019).
Our read: record tourism and low rates kept outside demand steady. Meanwhile Oʻahu households were leaving, mostly for Las Vegas, the West Coast and Texas, which fits the cost-of-living gap shown in section 08.
2020The shutdown
  • The 14-day travel quarantine cut air arrivals 74%, from 10.24 million to 2.68 million.
  • Foreign purchases fell 55%, to 243 from 540 a year earlier. Mainland purchases fell 14%, to 3,225 (17.4%), the low of the last decade.
  • Total sales dipped to 18,549. Rates fell to a 3.11% average.
Our read: the quarantine made visiting hard until pre-travel testing began on Oct 15, 2020. Outside purchases fell while locals kept buying on record-low rates, so the local share rose to 81%.
2021The rush
  • A record 25,970 sales, up 40%. Mainland purchases jumped 80% to 5,806, paying an average $1.20M.
  • Rates averaged 2.96%, the 20-year low.
  • Inventory fell by more than half: months of supply dropped to about one month on every island.
  • The Bay Area was the #2 source of new households from 2019–20 through 2021–22. The rise began just before the pandemic. Households moving to Maui in 2021–22 reported an average $192K in income, about double the usual level.
  • On Maui, buyers without a homeowner exemption (second homes, investors and landlords) peaked at 64.5% of home sales in FY2022 (July 2021–June 2022).
Our read: remote work, record-low mortgage rates of about 3% and stock-market wealth arrived together. High-income West Coast households could live and work here, and they bought faster than sellers listed.
2022–2023Rate shock and the fire
  • Rates jumped from 2.96% to 5.33% (2022) and 6.80% (2023). Sales fell to 21,131, then 15,638.
  • The mainland share held at 22–23%. From 2021 to 2023, mainland purchases fell 38% and local purchases fell 41%.
  • The Lahaina fire on Aug 8, 2023 destroyed most of the town. Maui County air arrivals fell from 2.97 million (2022) to 2.53 million (2023).
Our read: higher rates froze local owners in place, because few would trade a 3% mortgage for a 7% one. Mainland buying is far less sensitive to rates (see the model in section 14), so its share held.
2024–2025The locked-in market
  • Sales recovered to 18,007 in 2024, then slipped to 16,909 in 2025. Mainland purchases fell from 4,110 to 3,306, a 19.6% share.
  • On Maui the mainland share fell to 25%, the lowest in the 2015–2025 series, and condo prices fell sharply Reported, as condo insurance costs rose and Maui moved to phase out apartment-zoned vacation rentals (Bill 9, signed Dec 15, 2025, with deadlines in 2029 and 2031).
  • The mortgage payment on a median house took 66% of median income on Oʻahu and 78% on Maui in 2025.
Our read: rates stayed between 6.6% and 6.7% while prices held, so the monthly payment on a median house rose 75% on Oʻahu and 96% on Maui from 2021 to 2025. Oʻahu's 90-day rental minimum (2022) and Maui's pending Bill 9 made vacation-rental investing riskier, and HOA and insurance costs rose.
2026 to dateRates climb again
  • The 30-year rate rose from 6.05% (February average) to 7.28% on Oct 1, after six straight weekly increases.
  • Oʻahu's single-family median set a record of $1,242,500 in June, then fell to $1,110,000 in September, down 3.9% from a year earlier Reported.
  • Months of supply (August): Maui 9.6, Kauaʻi 8.4, Big Island 6.5, Oʻahu 5.2. That is buyers' territory on three of four islands.
  • January–June 2026, statewide: 7,726 recorded sales, up 1.4% (Title Guaranty, which counts all recorded deeds). Buyers included 552 from California, 175 from Washington and 138 from Japan Reported. MLS sales for the same months were down 1.0% (DBEDT).
Our read: rates fell through 2025 to a 6.05% low in February 2026, then rose instead. New listings are down from a year ago on Maui, Kauaʻi and the Big Island, but homes are selling more slowly, so inventory keeps building and the advantage is moving toward buyers.

  • Buyers come from the coastal cities. The Bay Area (net +3,496 households), Los Angeles–Orange County (+3,231), Chicago (+1,082) and New York (+890) send Hawaiʻi more households than they take, and those arrivals earn far more than the people leaving for those cities. Bay Area arrivals averaged $106K against $61K for leavers. California as a whole sent 6,270 more households than it took over 12 years.
  • Sellers go where housing is cheaper. Las Vegas took 12,894 more Hawaiʻi households than it sent. It is the #1 destination from every island. Next come the Texas metros (−4,659), Seattle–Tacoma (−4,257), Portland (−2,909) and Phoenix (−2,199).
  • The interior West is the growing exit. Departures per year to Phoenix rose 44%, Salt Lake City 43%, Denver 41%, Seattle 40%, Texas 34%, Portland 33% and Las Vegas 32% (2004–08 vs 2019–23). Departures to the Bay Area fell 29%.
  • Buyers who keep a mainland address are not in these counts. Second-home buyers show up in sales records instead. In 2025 the top states by number of purchases were California (1,271), Washington (278), Texas (175), Florida (159) and Colorado (136). The top countries were Japan (213), Korea (87) and Canada (64) Reported (Title Guaranty buyer stats via search extracts).
The two lists are a broker's two pipelines. Feeder metros are where to find buyers. Landing metros are where to build referral partnerships, because that is where Hawaiʻi sellers will buy their next home.

07 · Who is leaving, and who replaces them

Oʻahu has lost households almost every year for two decades. On the neighbor islands, richer arrivals are replacing departing residents.

IslandArrived, 19 yrsLeft, 19 yrsNetArrivals' income 2022–23Leavers' income 2022–23
  • Oʻahu: net −45,422 households over 19 years, losing in 16 of 19 years. Military rotation is part of it, but leaving has outnumbered arriving every year since 2015–16.
  • Neighbor islands: over 19 years the Big Island gained 9,004 households, Maui 2,903 and Kauaʻi 1,694. Since 2020 Maui and Kauaʻi have tipped into small losses (Maui −401 in 2022–23).
  • Income gap: the typical gap between arriving and departing households widened after 2011. On Maui it went from $2K to $22K, on Kauaʻi from $4K to $27K, and on the Big Island from $11K to $22K (median by year, before 2011 vs since). Since 2011–12 arrivals out-earned leavers on every neighbor island every year except Kauaʻi in 2018–19. On Oʻahu the two are about equal.

One in six homes on Maui and Kauaʻi is held for seasonal or occasional use

Log-linear fit of annual sales (DBEDT buyer-origin series) on the annual average Freddie Mac rate, 2015–2025. Eleven points including the 2020–21 pandemic years, so treat this as a rough guide. Mainland buying moves with incomes and wealth on the mainland, not with rates.

At UHERO's expected 2027 rate of about 6.7%, the model points to about 17,100 sales statewide (roughly 13,500 to 20,700). UHERO expects Oʻahu's single-family median to rise about 1.5% in 2027 and condos about 0.3% Reported (UHERO Q3 2026 forecast, via Star-Advertiser).

If rates fall below 6%

A smaller 2021

Local buyers return first, because they are the rate-sensitive group. Expect sales near 18,000 or more and inventory to absorb quickly. Mainland demand follows the stock market more than rates.

Do now: build buyer lists in the feeder metros.

Base case · rates 6.5–7%

A slow buyer's market

Sales stay near 16,000–18,000. The mainland share runs 19–23%, since cash buyers barely react to rates. Prices are flat in nominal terms and down after inflation. Maui, Kauaʻi and Big Island condos soften most, while Oʻahu single-family holds.

Do now: work high-income feeder metros, and run listing outreach to out-of-state owners.

If rates rise above 7.5%

Volume falls further

The 2023 pattern repeats: sales fall toward 15,000, price cuts spread, and the mainland share rises as locals drop out. Maui condo owners facing the Bill 9 deadlines and rising HOA costs become motivated sellers.

Do now: seller outreach, and cash buyers from feeder metros.

What to watch, with dates

  • Weekly: the Freddie Mac rate, every Thursday. It moves local demand the most.
  • Late 2026 or 2027: IRS 2023–24 migration data, the first year to show where households moved after the Lahaina fire.
  • Quarterly: Title Guaranty buyer stats by state and country.
  • Jan 1, 2029 and Jan 1, 2031: Maui Bill 9 deadlines for apartment-zoned vacation rentals (West Maui, then the rest of the county). Lawsuits are pending Reported.
  • Currencies: a yen near 1986 lows has turned Japanese owners into sellers. Canadian buying is weak after the 2025 travel boycott Reported.

When to run each campaign

  • Mainland buyer ads: June–July, December and March, the months when mainland visitors are on island (8–20% above an average month). January and February are 9–11% below.
  • International buyer outreach: December–March and August, the international visitor peaks.
  • Listings: go live by February–March. Closings are busiest March–June on every island (2–20% above an average month). January, February and November are the slowest, 8–23% below.
  • Military buyers: spring–summer PCS season (Oʻahu).
16 · Rules of the road

ZIP-level precision lives in the mail, not the ad platforms

Meta (FB/IG)
Housing ads go under the special ad category. There is no ZIP targeting, and locations expand to at least a 15-mile radius. Age must stay 18–65+ with all genders. No lookalike audiences. You can target origin cities and states and upload your own client list. Reported
Google
Under the housing policy, there is no targeting by ZIP, age, gender, parental status or marital status. Use city, metro, state or radius. Reported
Fair housing
Targeting by place is fine when it adds reach. Targeting by country, language or ancestry can read as a national-origin preference (42 USC 3604(c); HRS §515-3). Keep ad copy about the property, never about the buyer.
Direct mail
Owner mailing addresses in county tax records are public under Hawaiʻi's records law (OIP Op. Ltr. 11-01) Reported. Fair-housing and brokerage-name rules still apply.
Referrals
Referral fees to out-of-state agents must go through licensed brokerages, under each state's licensing rules.
Every ad
HAR §16-99-11 requires the brokerage's legal or registered trade name and the licensee's status.
Lahaina
Unsolicited offers to burn-zone owners were banned by the governor's Aug 2023 proclamation. Do not prospect there. Reported

A summary of rules, not legal advice. Confirm campaigns with your principal broker.
