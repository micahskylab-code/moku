01 · The decade at a glance

Outside buyers held a steady fifth of the market, then rode the 2021 surge

Locals buy three of every four Hawaiʻi homes in every year of the series. The mainland share ran about 19% before the pandemic, jumped to 22–23% from 2021 to 2024, and fell back to 19.6% in 2025. Foreign buying peaked in 2018 and has not recovered.

Over the decade, Maui's mainland share fell from 42% to 25%, while Oʻahu's rose from 10% to 14%. Outside demand moved toward Oʻahu and the Big Island and away from Maui.
02 · What happened, and why

Five chapters, each with a cause you can see in the data

2015–2019The steady climb
  • About 19,400–20,500 sales a year. Mainland buyers took 3,698–3,956 of them, between 18.6% and 19.4%.
  • Foreign buying peaked in 2018 at 928 sales (4.5%), averaging $1.25M.
  • Mortgage rates averaged 3.65–4.54%. Statewide visitor arrivals reached 10.24 million in 2019.
  • Maui's mainland share slid from 42% to 28% as prices rose. Kauaʻi and the Big Island held at 32–40%.
Why: cheap money and record tourism kept a steady flow of West Coast buyers coming. The IRS records show Los Angeles–Orange County, San Diego, Seattle and the Bay Area as the top four origin metros in every one of these years.
2020The shutdown
  • The 14-day travel quarantine cut statewide arrivals 74%, from 10.24 million to 2.68 million.
  • Foreign purchases collapsed to 243, from 540 a year earlier. Mainland purchases fell to 3,225 (17.4%), the decade low.
  • Total sales dipped to 18,549. Rates fell to a 3.11% average.
Why: buyers could not visit. Locals kept buying on record-low rates, so the local share rose to 81%.
2021The rush
  • A record 25,970 sales, up 40%. Mainland purchases jumped 80% to 5,806, paying an average $1.20M.
  • Rates averaged 2.96%, the decade low.
  • Inventory disappeared. Maui's active listings averaged 487, down from 1,020 in 2020, and hit 316 in November 2021. Kauaʻi bottomed at 127 in April 2022.
  • The Bay Area became the #2 source of new households (2019–20 through 2021–22). Households moving to Maui in 2021–22 averaged $192K in income, more than double the decade's usual level.
Why: remote work, near-zero rates and stock-market wealth arrived together. High-income West Coast households could live and work here, and they bought faster than sellers listed.
2022–2023Rate shock and the fire
  • Rates jumped from 2.96% to a 5.33% average in 2022 and 6.80% in 2023. Sales fell to 21,131, then 15,638, the decade low.
  • The mainland share held at 22–23%. Mainland buyers, many paying cash, pulled back less than locals.
  • On Maui, buyers who could not claim the homeowner exemption (second-home buyers and investors) peaked at 64.5% of home sales in FY2022 and 70.7% of dollar value.
  • The Lahaina fire on Aug 8, 2023 destroyed most of the town. Maui visitor arrivals fell from 2.97 million in 2022 to 2.53 million in 2023.
Why: higher rates froze local owners in place, because few would trade a 3% mortgage for a 7% one. Cash-rich outside buyers kept their share, and Maui's recovery after the fire has tilted toward housing residents.
2024–2025The locked-in market
  • Sales recovered to 18,007 in 2024, then slipped to 16,909 in 2025.
  • Mainland purchases fell from 4,110 to 3,306, a 19.6% share. Maui reached a decade low of 25%, and the Big Island fell from 39% to 32%.
  • Maui condos fell hard: the 2025 median dropped 23% to $692,860 Reported (Realtors Association of Maui), as condo-insurance costs and the Bill 9 vacation-rental phase-out hit.
  • Inventory rebuilt. Oʻahu averaged 3,256 active listings in 2025, against 1,649 in 2022. Maui averaged 1,301, against 419.
Why: rates stayed between 6.6% and 6.7% on average. Short-term-rental rules tightened on Maui and Oʻahu, and condo HOA and insurance costs rose. Those shocks hit the investor and second-home condo buyer, who is disproportionately from out of state.
2026 to dateRates climb again
  • The 30-year rate rose from 6.05% (February average) to 7.28% on Oct 1, after six straight weekly increases.
  • Oʻahu's single-family median set a record of $1,242,500 in June, then fell to $1,110,000 in September, down 3.9% from a year earlier Reported (Honolulu Board of Realtors via Star-Advertiser).
  • Listings are up from a year ago on every island as of September: Big Island +24.9%, Kauaʻi +8.1%, Oʻahu +6.4%, Maui +5.2%.
  • Sales through August are mixed. Maui condos are up 17%, Oʻahu single-family homes up 5.9%, and Kauaʻi condos down 26.6%.
Why: buyers expected rates to keep falling, and they rose instead. Sellers are listing anyway, so the advantage is slowly moving toward buyers, fastest on the Big Island.

04 · Where they came from

Nine years of IRS records: the same six metros, with the Bay Area peaking in the remote-work years

The IRS tracks every household that changes its tax address from one county to another. This is the only official source that names the mainland county a household came from. It covers people who moved here, not second-home buyers who kept a mainland address. Primary

  • Money comes from the north and west. King County (Seattle) households moving to Maui and the Big Island averaged $202K–206K in 2022–23. Santa Clara, Alameda and San Francisco households moving to Maui averaged $197K–283K across the last two years.
  • Volume comes from the south. San Diego, Las Vegas and Phoenix send the most households, at $52K–90K average income for most island pairings. Much of that flow goes to Oʻahu and is military.
  • Oʻahu is a military market. Base counties rank high on Oʻahu every year: Pierce WA (Lewis-McChord), Bexar TX, El Paso CO and TX, Bell TX, Cumberland NC. In 2022–23 a further 996 households came from military mail addresses (APO/FPO).
  • Las Vegas runs both ways. In 2022–23, 1,243 households left Oʻahu for Clark County and 523 came back. The "ninth island" is where many local families go, and some return as buyers.

07 · Where it goes next

2027 outlook: flat prices, slow volume, and a better market for patient buyers

This section is judgment, built on the record above and UHERO's latest forecast. It is not fact. Forecast

UHERO (University of Hawaiʻi Economic Research Organization) now expects the 30-year rate to average about 6.7% in 2027 and 6.6% in 2028. It expects Oʻahu's single-family median to rise about 1.5% in 2027 to roughly $1.2M, and condos about 0.3%. Honolulu inflation reached 5.6% in the 12 months to July 2026. Reported (UHERO Q3 2026 forecast, via Star-Advertiser)

If rates fall below 6%

A smaller 2021

The record shows mainland buyers react first and hardest when money gets cheap: purchases rose 80% in 2021. Expect West Coast second-home demand to return to Maui and Kauaʻi resort areas within two or three quarters, with inventory absorbing quickly.

Do now: build the West Coast lists before it happens.

Base case · rates 6.5–7%

A slow buyer's market

Sales stay near 16,000–18,000 a year. The mainland share stays around 19–22%, because cash buyers are less rate-sensitive. Prices are flat in nominal terms and down after inflation. Neighbor-island condos and the Big Island soften most, while Oʻahu single-family holds.

Do now: target cash-heavy, high-income origins (Bay Area, Seattle, Orange County) and listing pipelines from out-of-state owners.

If rates rise above 7.5%

Volume falls further

The 2023 pattern repeats: sales fall toward 15,000, price cuts spread, and the mainland share rises again as locals drop out. Distressed sellers emerge among Maui condo owners facing the Bill 9 deadlines and rising HOA costs.

Do now: seller outreach to out-of-state owners, and cash buyers for the deals.

What to watch, with dates

  • Weekly: the Freddie Mac rate, published every Thursday. It is the single biggest driver in the record.
  • Late 2026 or 2027: IRS 2023–24 migration data, the first year to show where households moved after the Lahaina fire.
  • Quarterly: Title Guaranty buyer stats by state and country, the earliest read on mainland and foreign share.
  • Jan 1, 2029 and Jan 1, 2031: Maui Bill 9 deadlines for ending apartment-zoned vacation rentals in West Maui and the rest of the county. Lawsuits are pending. Expect condo listings to build ahead of each date.
  • Currencies: the yen near 1986 lows has turned Japanese owners into sellers. A weak Canadian dollar and the 2025 travel boycott have held back Canadian buyers.

Why each group buys

Largest outside segment

West Coast second-home buyer

Affluent, 45–64, often paying cash. They buy on the neighbor islands, in resort condos and homes. Much less rate-sensitive than locals: their share rose in 2022–23 while total sales fell.

Bay Area, Seattle, Orange County. Mainland winter (Oct–Feb), while they are on island.
Fixed cycle

Military buyer

Base-county inflows to Oʻahu are large and steady every year. These buyers use VA loans, and the home often becomes a rental after the next assignment.

San Diego, Pierce WA, Bexar TX, El Paso CO/TX. PCS season (spring–summer).
Coming home

Returning kamaʻāina

Las Vegas runs both ways: far more Oʻahu households leave for Clark County than return, but several hundred return every year.

Las Vegas–Henderson, Southern California. Hawaiʻi clubs, alumni networks, holiday homecomings.
Retiree

Pension retiree

Hawaiʻi does not tax Social Security or employer-funded pensions Reported. Phoenix, Portland and Salt Lake City flows lean older and mid-income.

Lead with the tax treatment. Single-level condos near care.
Being reshaped by law

Rental investor

Maui's Bill 9 (Ordinance 5909) ends apartment-zoned vacation rentals by 2029 in West Maui and 2031 elsewhere Reported. Investor demand moves to legal resort zones. Existing condo owners become sellers.

Out-of-state Maui condo owners (direct mail). 1031 buyers.
Currency-driven

Japanese and Canadian owners

The share of sales going to foreign buyers fell from 4.5% (2018) to 2.5% (2025) Official. A weak yen and Canadian dollar favor selling over buying.

Listing outreach to foreign owners of Oʻahu and Maui condos.
09 · Rules of the road

ZIP-level precision lives in the mail, not the ad platforms

Meta (FB/IG)
Housing ads go under the special ad category. There is no ZIP targeting, and locations expand to at least a 15-mile radius. Age must stay 18–65+ with all genders. No lookalike audiences. You can target origin cities and states and upload your own client list. Reported
Google
Under the housing policy, there is no targeting by ZIP, age, gender, parental status or marital status. Use city, metro, state or radius. Reported
Fair housing
Targeting by place is fine when it adds reach. Targeting by country, language or ancestry can read as a national-origin preference (42 USC 3604(c); HRS §515-3). Keep ad copy about the property, never about the buyer.
Direct mail
Owner mailing addresses in county tax records are public under Hawaiʻi's records law (OIP Op. Ltr. 11-01) Reported. Mail can go to any ZIP. Fair-housing and brokerage-name rules still apply.
Every ad
HAR §16-99-11 requires the brokerage's legal or registered trade name and the licensee's status.
Lahaina
Unsolicited offers to burn-zone owners were banned by the governor's Aug 2023 proclamation. Do not prospect there. Reported

A summary of rules, not legal advice. Confirm campaigns with your principal broker. Items tagged Reported are being re-verified against their sources.
