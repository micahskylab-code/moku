The short answer

  • Prices outran incomes. Oʻahu's median house price rose 80% from 2006 to 2025. In 2025 the mortgage payment on a median house took 66% of a typical Oʻahu income, 78% on Maui and 76% on Kauaʻi, up from about 40% in 2019. Higher rates did most of that.
  • Buyers come mostly from California. Mainland buyers make about a fifth of purchases statewide, but about half the purchase dollars on the Big Island and Kauaʻi. California is the #1 out-of-state buyer state on every island; Washington, Colorado, Oregon, Alaska, Texas and Florida follow, depending on the island.
  • Hawaiʻi's families go to Las Vegas. The state lost 31,821 more households to other states than it gained in 19 years, all of it from Oʻahu. Las Vegas (Clark County) is the #1 destination county from every island. Denver, Salt Lake City and Portland are the fastest-growing exits.
  • Every island is different. Locals buy most homes under $3 million. Above it, mainland and foreign buyers take a third of Oʻahu sales, and buyers without a homeowner exemption take 81–89% of Maui sales. Condos are the outsiders' market: about half of neighbor-island condo buyers are from out of state. The Big Island now leads at $10M+.
  • The market has turned toward buyers. Rates are back above 7%. Maui, Kauaʻi and the Big Island have 6.5–9.6 months of supply (August 2026). Oʻahu is balanced overall at 5.2 months, but its houses have only about 3, still a seller's market. So far in 2026, Oʻahu house sales are up 6%, and Maui condo sales are up 21% at prices 7% lower.
  • The playbook. For the neighbor islands, the biggest feeder is Oʻahu. On the mainland, find buyers in the Bay Area, Los Angeles–Orange County and Seattle, and second-home buyers in Salt Lake City, Denver, Minneapolis and Anchorage. Build referral partnerships in Las Vegas, Phoenix and Texas, where departing Hawaiʻi households go.

Twenty years at a glance

Two booms, a crash, and a market that now takes far more of local income

For brokersPrices are near records, and the monthly payment on a median house rose 75% on Oʻahu and 96% on Maui from 2021 to 2025. Expect fewer deals, longer listing times and more negotiation.
What happened, and why

Nine chapters: what the data shows, and our read of why

2006–2007The top of the first boom
  • Oʻahu recorded 10,421 sales in 2006, a level not seen again until 2021. Maui recorded 2,331.
  • Oʻahu's price index peaked in 2006 at 95.3 (2015 = 100). Maui's single-family median averaged $702K that year.
  • Mortgage rates averaged 6.41% and 6.34%, close to 2026's year-to-date average of 6.49%. The state authorized 7,549 new homes in 2006, the most in 20 years.
  • The neighbor islands were gaining households: in 2004–05 the Big Island took in 1,126 more households from other states than it lost.
  • On Maui, 37% of 2004 home sales went to buyers living outside Maui County, and more than half in West Maui Reported (County of Maui Planning, 2006). No official statewide series by buyer origin starts before 2008.
Our read: easy credit and a national housing boom. Mainland households were moving to the neighbor islands (IRS shows net gains there), while Oʻahu was already losing slightly more households than it gained.
2008–2011The crash
  • Sales fell 40% on Oʻahu from 2006 to 2009 (to 6,263) and 34% on Maui (to 1,528).
  • Maui's single-family median fell 37%, from $702K (2006) to $439K (2011). On the same measure Oʻahu's fell 9%, from $632K to $576K; its repeat-sales index fell 14%, from 95.3 to 82.3.
  • New-home permits collapsed to 2,592 in 2009. Rates fell from 6.04% (2008) to 4.46% (2011).
  • Mainland buyers' share of recorded sales rose from 23.5% (2008) to 29.3% in 2010 (6,207 purchases), the high of the 2008–2025 series. That year recorded sales jumped 31% while MLS sales rose 19%, so read the 2010 peak with caution. Foreign buyers' share reached 5.0% in 2011.
  • On the neighbor islands, migration nearly stopped: the net flow was a few hundred households a year either way. Visitor arrivals by air bottomed at 6.42 million in 2009.
Our read: the national financial crisis. Prices fell much harder on Maui (−37% median) than on Oʻahu (−9% median, −14% repeat-sales index). Maui depends more on outside buyers and visitors, while on Oʻahu the military and government base steadied demand, even though Oʻahu's sales count fell slightly more.
2012–2015Recovery, and the foreign buyer
  • Rates hit 3.66% in 2012. Oʻahu's price index rose 21%, from 82.3 (2011) to 100 (2015).
  • International visitors rose from 2.05 million (2011) to 2.78 million (2015). Foreign buyers' share was 4.9% in 2012, then fell to 3.0% by 2015. The mainland share held near 21% in 2012–2014, then fell to 19% in 2015.
  • The neighbor islands gained households again, and the arrivals were richer. On Kauaʻi in 2012–13, households moving in averaged $103K in income, against $47K for those leaving.
Our read: cheap money, a strong yen (2011–12) and a Canadian dollar near par drew Asian and Canadian buyers. As the yen weakened from 2013, the foreign share fell. Higher-income mainland households began replacing departing locals on the neighbor islands.
2016–2019The steady climb
  • About 19,800–20,500 sales a year. Mainland buyers took 3,702–3,956 of them, between 18.6% and 19.4%.
  • Foreign purchases reached their highest count in 2018, 928 (4.5%), averaging $1.25M. Japanese buyers made 610 purchases that year Reported (Title Guaranty, via Hawaii Business).
  • Mortgage rates averaged 3.65–4.54%. Visitor arrivals by air reached 10.24 million in 2019.
  • Oʻahu started losing 3,300–7,700 households a year to other states. Maui's mainland buyer share slid from 42% (2015) to 28% (2019).
Our read: record tourism and low rates kept outside demand steady. Meanwhile Oʻahu households were leaving, mostly for Las Vegas, the West Coast and Texas, which fits the cost-of-living gap shown under The squeeze on local families.
2020The shutdown
  • The 14-day travel quarantine cut air arrivals 74%, from 10.24 million to 2.68 million.
  • Foreign purchases fell 55%, to 243 from 540 a year earlier. Mainland purchases fell 14%, to 3,225 (17.4%), the lowest count and share since at least 2008.
  • Total sales dipped to 18,549. Rates fell to a 3.11% average.
Our read: the quarantine made visiting hard until pre-travel testing began on Oct 15, 2020. Outside purchases fell while locals kept buying on record-low rates, so the local share rose to 81%.
2021The rush
  • A record 25,970 sales, up 40%. Mainland purchases jumped 80% to 5,806, paying an average $1.20M.
  • Rates averaged 2.96%, the 20-year low.
  • Inventory fell by more than half: months of supply dropped to about one month on every island.
  • The Bay Area was the #2 source of new households from 2019–20 through 2021–22. The rise began just before the pandemic. Households moving to Maui in 2021–22 reported an average $192K in income, about double the usual level.
  • On Maui, buyers without a homeowner exemption (second homes, investors and landlords) peaked at 64.5% of home sales in FY2022 (July 2021–June 2022).
Our read: remote work, record-low mortgage rates of about 3% and stock-market wealth arrived together. High-income West Coast households could live and work here, and they bought faster than sellers listed.
2022–2023Rate shock and the fire
  • Rates jumped from 2.96% to 5.33% (2022) and 6.80% (2023). Recorded sales fell to 21,131, then 15,638, the fewest in the 2008–2025 recorded series. MLS sales (11,997 in 2023) stayed above their 2008–2011 levels.
  • The mainland share held at 22–23%. From 2021 to 2023, mainland purchases fell 38% and local purchases fell 41%.
  • The Lahaina fire on Aug 8, 2023 destroyed most of the town. Maui County air arrivals fell from 2.97 million (2022) to 2.53 million (2023).
Our read: higher rates froze local owners in place, because few would trade a 3% mortgage for a 7% one. Mainland purchases fell almost as much as local ones (38% vs 41%), so the mainland share held.
2024–2025The locked-in market
  • DBEDT counts 18,007 sales in 2024 and 16,909 in 2025. Its 2024 figure runs about 8% above Title Guaranty's own 2024 count (16,709), while the two agree for 2023 and 2025; on Title Guaranty's count, 2025 sales rose 1.2%. The mainland share was 19.6% in 2025, against 22.8% in 2024.
  • On Maui the mainland share fell to 25% in 2025, the lowest in the 2015–2025 county series, and condo prices fell sharply Reported, as condo insurance costs rose and Maui moved to phase out apartment-zoned vacation rentals (Bill 9, signed Dec 15, 2025, with deadlines in 2029 and 2031).
  • The mortgage payment on a median house took 66% of median income on Oʻahu and 78% on Maui in 2025.
Our read: rates stayed between 6.6% and 6.7% while prices held, so the monthly payment on a median house rose 75% on Oʻahu and 96% on Maui from 2021 to 2025. Oʻahu's 90-day rental minimum (2022) and Maui's pending Bill 9 made vacation-rental investing riskier, and HOA and insurance costs rose.
2026 to dateRates climb again
  • The 30-year rate rose from 6.05% (February average) to 7.28% on Oct 1, after six straight weekly increases.
  • Oʻahu's single-family median set a record of $1,242,500 in June, then fell to $1,110,000 in September, down 3.9% from a year earlier Reported.
  • Months of supply (August): Maui 9.6, Kauaʻi 8.4, Big Island 6.5, Oʻahu 5.2. That is buyers' territory on three of four islands.
  • January–June 2026, statewide: 7,738 recorded sales, up 1.5% from a year earlier. The mainland share eased to 19.2% (21.8%) while foreign buyers rose to 2.9% (2.3%). Oʻahu sales rose 9%, the Big Island fell 9% and Kauaʻi 34% Official (DBEDT quarterly report). Buyers included 552 from California, 175 from Washington and 138 from Japan Reported. MLS sales for the same months were down 1.0% (DBEDT).
Our read: rates fell through 2025 to a 6.05% low in February 2026, then rose instead. New listings are down from a year ago on Maui, Kauaʻi and the Big Island, but homes are selling more slowly, so inventory keeps building and the advantage is moving toward buyers.

Rules of the road

ZIP-level precision lives in the mail, not the ad platforms

Meta (FB/IG)
Housing ads go under the special ad category. No targeting by ZIP code, age or gender: audiences must include ages 18–65+ and all genders, and a city or address expands to at least a 15-mile radius in the U.S. and Canada (15 km elsewhere). No location exclusions or lookalike audiences. You can target origin cities and states, and use your own client list if Meta marks it eligible.
Google
Under the housing policy (U.S. and Canada), there is no targeting by ZIP, age, gender, parental status or marital status. Use city, metro, state or radius.
Fair housing
Ads that indicate a preference based on national origin, ancestry, race or other protected classes are illegal (42 USC 3604(c); HRS §515-3), and targeting by place can act as a proxy for them. Base geographic targeting on documented demand, keep it broad, and have country-level or language-specific campaigns reviewed. Keep ad copy about the property, never about the buyer. HUD withdrew its 2024 guidance on digital housing ads in September 2025, but the law itself is unchanged.
Direct mail
Can go to any ZIP except 96761, 96767 and 96790 (see Lahaina and Kula). Owner mailing addresses in county tax records are public under Hawaiʻi's records law (OIP Op. Ltr. 11-01); Maui County offers a free owner and mailing-address extract. Fair-housing and brokerage-name rules still apply.
Referrals
Referral fees to out-of-state agents must go through licensed brokerages, under each state's licensing rules.
Every ad
HAR §16-99-11 requires the brokerage's legal or registered trade name and the licensee's status.
Lahaina and Kula
The governor's wildfire emergency proclamations ban unsolicited offers to buy any real property from any owner in ZIP codes 96761, 96767 and 96790 (Lahaina–Kāʻanapali–Kapalua, Lahaina post office boxes, and Kula). A violation is a misdemeanor: up to a $5,000 fine and a year in jail. The ban has been renewed in every proclamation since August 2023; the latest checked, the Thirty-second (August 24, 2026), runs about 60 days, to around October 23, 2026. Check for a renewal before relying on its lapse, and do not prospect there.

A summary of rules, not legal advice. Confirm campaigns with your principal broker.
