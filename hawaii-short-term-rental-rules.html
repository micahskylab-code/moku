Maui County · Maui, Molokaʻi, Lānaʻi

Maui County

Vacation rentals are allowed in hotel- and resort-zoned areas, permitted bed-and-breakfasts, and licensed short-term rental homes in some single-family zones. The contested category is the “Minatoya List” — roughly 7,000 apartment-zoned condos that ran as legal vacation rentals for about 40 years under a grandfathered zoning exemption.

The headline change — Bill 9. Signed into law December 15, 2025, Bill 9 phases out vacation-rental use in those apartment-zoned “Minatoya” condos — about 6,200 units the County reported actively operating. It is in effect but under active court challenge (two lawsuits filed in late 2025); as of this writing no court has paused it. Hotel- and resort-zoned rentals are unaffected. The phase-out deadlines are set by the ordinance and are reported to begin around 2029 in West Maui and 2031 elsewhere — confirm the exact dates with Maui County Planning before relying on them.
Source: Maui County · Bill 9 (2025) · verify current dates with the County Planning Department.

City & County of Honolulu · Oʻahu

Oʻahu

Rentals under 30 days are allowed as-of-right mainly in resort-zoned areas (Waikīkī, Ko Olina, Turtle Bay, Hoʻakalei), plus a fixed pool of grandfathered nonconforming-use certificates (NUCs) — roughly 800 of them, with no new ones issued since about 1990. They are prohibited in ordinary residential neighborhoods unless grandfathered.

The framework is Bill 41 (2021), Ordinance 22-7, which created registered “B&B” (hosted) and “TVU” (whole-home) categories and sought to raise the residential minimum stay from 30 to 90 days.

The 90-day fight. A federal court blocked the 90-day rule (the HILSTRA case), so the enforceable minimum for pre-existing residential rentals remains 30 days. A 2025 ordinance again seeking 90 days faces the same precedent and remains legally contested.
Source: Honolulu Dept. of Planning & Permitting · Ordinance 22-7 · confirm registration status with DPP.

Kauaʻi County

Kauaʻi

Transient vacation rentals are allowed by right inside Visitor Destination Areas (VDAs) — the resort/visitor zones around Poʻipū, Princeville and the Kapaʻa–Wailua coast. Outside a VDA, a rental is legal only if it holds a grandfathered nonconforming-use certificate. “Transient” means 180 days or less.

The pool is closed. New nonconforming permits ended in 2009 — none are issued today. Existing certificates must be renewed every year (about $750) with proof that GET and TAT taxes are current, and a single missed renewal permanently ends the use (zero tolerance). The County keeps a public, TMK-keyed registry of approved nonconforming rentals and homestays.
Source: Kauaʻi County Planning — Transient Vacation Rentals.

Hawaiʻi County · Big Island

Hawaiʻi Island

Short-term vacation rentals are permitted in resort, hotel, commercial and multifamily/apartment zones, and prohibited in single-family residential and agricultural zones unless grandfathered with a nonconforming-use certificate. The zoning framework is Bill 108 (2018), Ordinance 2018-114.

New registration, still rolling out. Bill 47 (2025) adds a mandatory county registration for hosted and unhosted rentals, with fees reported around $250–$500 and fines for operating unregistered. The start date has slipped repeatedly — most recently to around September 2026 — and the portal was not yet open as of mid-2026. A further bill (147) that would broaden the rules remains pending. Confirm the current registration status with Hawaiʻi County Planning.
Source: Hawaiʻi County Planning — Short-Term Vacation Rentals.
