What the assembled record can already see

Not a mockup of future value — an original read of the Kauaʻi land market, produced from the records Moku already holds. This is the kind of thing the platform exists to make routine.

Exhibit · Kauaʻi land market · 2023 – H1 2026
The land market got smaller and far more expensive at the same time.

Kauaʻi recorded 110 land sales in 2023, 119 in 2024, and 92 in 2025 — a market shrinking in transaction count. Yet 2025 dollar volume went the other way: $157.5M → $190.0M, up 21% on 23% fewer sales.

That divergence has one arithmetic explanation: the average Kauaʻi land sale rose from $1.32M to $2.07M — up 56% in a single year. Fewer parcels traded, and the ones that did were a different class of dirt.

That last number is the one a developer cannot get anywhere else. In 2025 a single community — Kukuiʻula — closed 21 land parcels for $33.3M, per its own published year in review. Against an island that recorded 92 land sales and $190.0M in land volume, that is 23% of all transactions and 18% of all land dollars, absorbed at a pace of 1.75 lots per month.

Momentum turned in 2026: 14 land sales in Q1, 22 in Q2 — but at a lower H1 median of $841,000, against $950,000 for full-year 2025. Volume is recovering on cheaper dirt. For anyone pricing lots, financing a subdivision, or underwriting absorption on this island, that is the whole question — and it is answerable only because someone assembled the record.

Sources. Land sale counts, volumes and medians: Hawaii Information Service full-year figures (2025) and H1 2026 statistics published Aug 7, 2026; 2023–2024 from MLS-based island recaps. Community figures: that community's own published 2025 year in review. Averages, shares and absorption pace are Moku's arithmetic on those published figures, shown so they can be checked. Annualized 2026 doubles H1 and is labeled as an estimate, not a published figure.

What is not true yet

Written plainly, because you would find it anyway and because it is what we most need your read on.

  • No audience yet. The product is live and free, but it has not been launched or marketed. There is no traffic story to show you.
  • No revenue yet. Nothing is being sold today. The first intended sale is a monthly records memo to a land developer — the exhibit above is its first issue.
  • Solo founder, AI-built engineering. One person, with AI doing the building. That is a real key-person risk and it belongs in any valuation.
  • The founder's real-estate license is pending. Moku is deliberately a public-records publisher — not a brokerage. No valuations, no advice, no listings.
  • Two islands are dark. Kauaʻi and Hawaiʻi County publish no sales data at all; formal records requests for both are written and waiting to be filed. Rather than estimate, the map stays empty there.
